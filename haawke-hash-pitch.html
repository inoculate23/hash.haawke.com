The Provenance Problem

The FCC's foreign router ban created a new requirement overnight: manufacturers must prove, at the component level, where their hardware came from. Haawke Hash is the cryptographic infrastructure to make that proof permanent, independent, and verifiable by anyone — including the FCC, DHS, and the Department of Defense.

The Problem · As of March 23, 2026

The FCC's conditional approval process for foreign-made routers requires a detailed bill of materials, country of origin for every component, entities responsible for IP ownership and firmware, and justification for any foreign-manufactured parts. These documents must be submitted to DHS or the Department of Defense for national security review. Provenance that cannot be independently verified is provenance that can be challenged. A supply chain claim backed only by internal records is a liability. One backed by Bitcoin-anchored cryptographic timestamps is a fact.

How Haawke Hash Works
01
Document created
BOM · sourcing record · firmware build · ownership filing
Any digital document — a spreadsheet, a PDF, a JSON record — is submitted to Haawke Hash at the moment of creation.
02
SHA-256 fingerprint generated
hash.haawke.com · cryptographic fingerprint
A unique 256-bit fingerprint is generated from the document contents. Any change to the document — even a single character — produces a completely different hash. The fingerprint is mathematically bound to the document.
03
Bitcoin blockchain anchor
OpenTimestamps · Bitcoin blockchain · immutable timestamp
The hash is anchored to the Bitcoin blockchain via OpenTimestamps. This creates a permanent, global timestamp that cannot be altered retroactively. The document's existence at a specific point in time is now a fact recorded in the most tamper-resistant ledger in existence.
04
Public registry entry
verify.haawke.com · independently verifiable · no Haawke account required
The hash, timestamp, and record are entered into the public Haawke Verify registry. Any party — the FCC, DHS, DoD, a journalist, a competitor — can verify the record independently without accessing Haawke's systems or trusting Haawke's word.
Why This Matters for FCC Conditional Approval
Retroactive claims are unverifiable

If you assemble your provenance documentation after the fact, there is no way to prove when the records were created. A Bitcoin-anchored timestamp proves the document existed at a specific moment. That is the difference between a claim and a fact.

Independent verification removes trust requirements

The FCC, DHS, and DoD do not need to trust Haawke. They verify against the Bitcoin blockchain directly. The proof is mathematical, not institutional.

Audit trail is permanent and queryable

Every document in the chain — BOM, sourcing records, firmware builds, ownership filings — is permanently recorded. If a component origin is ever challenged, the record is there. It cannot be amended or deleted.

Onshoring progress is provable over time

As manufacturers shift production to US sources, each step can be documented and anchored. The FCC's conditional approval process is evolving into a certification program — provenance infrastructure that proves progress is a competitive advantage.
